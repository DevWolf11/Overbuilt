# Overbuilt

Build any machine from parts, then take it anywhere and do anything with it. The game mixes the rocketry of *Spaceflight Simulator* and *Kerbal Space Program*, the free-form aircraft building of *SimplePlanes*, and the combat of *War Thunder*. It is free, and it runs in a browser on a PC, a phone or a tablet, with nothing to install.

You attach parts together on a blueprint, in 2D or in 3D: rockets, planes, helicopters, cars, drones and armour (tanks, tank destroyers, armoured cars, SPAA, infantry vehicles, missile carriers, rocket artillery and SAM launchers), with engines, wings, tracks, turrets, weapons and paint wherever you want them. Then you fly or drive them in a full 3D world. Earth is the real one, shrunk to 1/20 scale, and the space centre sits on the Faroe Islands. From there you can:
- launch from the pad, reach orbit and explore a small solar system: land on the Moon, drive a rover, parachute onto Mars, dive past the Sun, and bring the crew home through a fiery reentry;
- fly planes that bank and turn over the islands and break the sound barrier, drive off across the islands, or hover a helicopter, and in Sandbox start them on Shetland or in Scotland as well as on the Faroes;
- drive a tank you built yourself onto Highland Ridge in North Scotland, where shells bounce off sloped plate, crews get knocked out and ammunition cooks off, in the spirit of War Thunder's ground battles;
- fight AI targets, fighters and computer-driven tanks, or other players online: in a free-for-all sandbox, in air battles where everyone builds on a budget and then fights, or in ground battles over capture zones with tickets and respawns.

It runs in any modern browser on desktop and mobile. There is no build step: open `index.html` and play. The 3D world uses three.js, which ships in the repo. On a device without WebGL the game falls back to a flat 2D side view (Armour then drives on the runway, and ground battles need the 3D view).

## Play

- **Online:** https://devwolf11.github.io/Overbuilt/ (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main` on every push). On a phone, use "Add to Home Screen" to install it and play offline.
- **Locally:** serve the folder (`npx serve .`). Offline play and installing need the HTTPS site above, because the service worker only registers over `https:`. Double-clicking `index.html` works too, but browsers don't let a page opened from disk read the Earth map, so Earth falls back to generated terrain.
- **Single file:** `node tools/build-single.mjs` writes `dist/overbuilt.html`, the whole game in one HTML file you can send to someone.

## Modes

- **Career:** start with $50,000 and basic parts. Every launch costs the price of its parts; recovering a craft on Earth refunds 90%. Complete 42 missions (first hop, orbit, flybys, orbits, landings and returns for every body, a Sun dive, interstellar escape, plus a first takeoff, a runway landing, the sound barrier, Mach 2, a seaplane round trip, a hover, a road trip by car or Armour, and three knock-outs on the Tank Range) to earn funds, and spend them in R&D to unlock five tiers of technology. A Practice Battle's vehicle is lent: it costs nothing and is never saved.
- **Sandbox:** every part unlocked and no money involved.
- **Multiplayer:** play with friends in your browsers, with no server and no accounts. See [Multiplayer](#multiplayer). The same screen has a **Practice battle (vs AI)**: a ground battle alone, with no room needed.

## Multiplayer

Press **Multiplayer** on the home screen, type a name, and either create a room or join one with its five-letter code. **Online** connects browsers directly to each other (WebRTC, through the free PeerJS service to find each other); **This device** joins other tabs of the same browser, which is handy for trying it alone or playing offline. The player who creates the room hosts it: their browser runs the match, and the room closes when they leave. The host's game lets friends in, so the host should keep it on screen while they join; on a phone it can't answer while the screen is off or another app is open (joining waits up to a minute for it to come back, says so, and the Join button turns into Cancel meanwhile). If joining fails, the message says at which step: no room with that code, the host's game not answering, or the two devices unable to connect (some routers, mobile networks, VPNs and school networks block direct links). **Test this network** on the multiplayer screen checks, in about 10 seconds, whether the matchmaking server, direct links and the relay server get through where you are. The matchmaking server is only needed to let people in: if the host loses it, the room plays on, the host is told once (and again when it's back), the room card says joining is paused, and the game keeps trying quietly in the background. On networks that block direct links (UDP), such as many school networks, play goes through a relay (TURN) server instead, over port 443 like a website. The game uses a free relay account (Metered's free plan, 20 GB a month; if it ever runs out, the relay stops until the next month and the test says so).

- **Sandbox** (up to 5 players): the normal world, every part, no rules. Build anything in the hangar and fly it into the shared sky; everyone sees everyone's craft, smoke and gunfire, and weapons work on each other. Crash, and you fly again or go back to the hangar. Everyone shares the host's clock, so the sun, the clouds and the time of day agree. A craft may have up to 600 parts; the host can turn that **part limit** off in the room's card (at any time in a sandbox, in the lobby before a battle), and then anything the network can send flies, about 5,000 parts, though big craft slow down phones. Armour, and cars armed with the ground update's guns or launchers, start side by side at the **tank yard** on the space centre's island (a concrete pad south-east of the runway, past garages and a fuel depot, facing a small driving course) when the Earth map is in and the view is 3D; any other car, armed or not, starts on the runway as before. Drones start at the helipad, and a tank with a drone bay launches its drone in the room too: it flies beside the parked tank as your second craft, and the others see it as "Ann's drone". The hangar's **Start** button works in a sandbox room too: pick one of the sandbox's spawn points (below) and you start there, a little apart from anyone else who picked it (the others simply see where you are).
- **Air battle** (1v1, 2v2, 3v3 or a six-player free-for-all; **Create air battle**): the host picks the format, the money ($45k, $75k or $120k), the build time (2, 3 or 5 minutes), whether there is a test flight, carriers, friendly fire, time of day and a time limit (10, 15 or 25 minutes). Then:
  1. **Build.** Everyone builds against the clock with the money they have; the stats show the cost against the budget. Air battles are for planes, helicopters and cars; ground battles (below) for Armour and cars, with aircraft only as air support. First pick a spawn on the map (War Thunder style): team Faroes starts on the space centre's runway or Carrier West, team Shetland at Sumburgh airfield on the southern tip of Shetland or Carrier East, and a free-for-all adds Wick airfield on the north coast of Scotland and Carrier South. A carrier gives 30% more money, but the craft has to fit the deck and the catapult: at most 13 m across, 16 m long and 12 tonnes, planes and helicopters only. Press Ready when you're done; the phase ends early once everyone is ready.
  2. **Test flight** (optional, one minute): fly your design alone, with nobody to shoot you, then get **two minutes to polish it**.
  3. **Battle.** Everyone spawns at once. One life each: the last team (or pilot) flying wins, and when the time runs out the side with the most craft still up wins, then the most kills. Shot down? Watch your teammates (`[` / `]`). A design that is over budget or breaks the carrier limits when the battle starts is swapped for a stock plane that fits.
- **Ground battle** (1v1, 2v2 or 3v3; **Create ground battle**, or Game: Ground in a battle's lobby): the War Thunder-style ground mode. The host picks the format, the money by era ($45k WWII, $75k Cold War, $120k Modern), the tickets (400, 600 or 900 a side), the respawns (Free, or a Lineup of 3), air support (on or off), the aim (Arcade or Realistic), then the build time, a test drive (a minute on your pad, then two to polish), friendly fire, time of day and the time limit. The arena is the host's map's: **Highland Ridge**, rolling ground in North Scotland measured from the real map with a village, farms, woods and walls to fight through (below), or the space centre's runway when the Earth map didn't load (sheds, containers and sandbags along its sides) (the lobby warns a player whose map differs from the host's). Ground battles need the 3D view.
  1. **Build** Armour or a car against the clock; with a Lineup of 3, pick two more vehicles for slots 2 and 3 (saved or stock designs). A design over the money when the battle starts is swapped for the era's stock tank, never a plane.
  2. **Fight.** Each team starts on its own levelled pad, the two 2.4 km apart and out of each other's sight, facing three zones: the Hilltop (A), which overlooks the others, the Slope (C) in the middle and the East ground (B). A tank or car under 30 m/s takes a neutral zone in 20 s alone, 1.5 times as fast with two, 1.8 with three or more, and half as fast again with living troops aboard; an enemy zone goes neutral at the halfway mark and is yours after 40 s. Both sides inside freeze it. The side holding fewer zones bleeds the difference in tickets every second, and every vehicle lost costs its team 30 tickets plus one for every $2,500 it cost (half as much again for an aircraft). Out of tickets, or with nobody left who can respawn, a team loses; on time, more tickets wins. A 1v1 is fought over zone C alone, 70 m across. Twelve earth berms give hull-down cover, and leaving the battle area brings a warning every 5 s.
  3. **Knocked out?** Your hulk stays where it stopped, on every screen, as a charred wreck that burns, then smokes, blocks shells and gives cover for five minutes, sinking into the ground at the end (at most eight standing: a ninth starts the oldest sinking; an ammunition detonation throws its turret, which goes with it). After 5 s the **Respawn** panel offers your vehicle, another of your designs or a stock one within the money, or, with air support on and a sortie point earned by knock-outs and captures, an aircraft (one a flight, a minute apart), which joins 1,000 m up at 140 m/s behind your pad. A Lineup of 3 offers the slots you haven't used. A fresh spawn can't be hurt for 4 s.
  - **Spotting:** an enemy's red tag shows only while you or a teammate can see it: within 250 m, in your line of sight within 1.5 km (a quarter further with a living commander; 3 km between the ground and the air) with hills, wrecks and smoke in the way (a Thermal Sight sees through smoke), for 3 s after a teammate sees it (15 s after `J`, or a long press on its tag), and for 3 s after it fires its main gun within 2.5 km. Aircraft see enemy aircraft within about 6 km (less if they are stealthy), or further while a radar part's scope holds them, so a radar plane spots enemy aircraft for its whole team. A tap on the tactical map pings a spot for your team.
  - **Drones:** a tank with a drone bay launches its drones in a battle too (`U`): the drone flies beside the parked tank, the enemy sees and shoots at it, and losing it costs no tickets (it is the tank's). A drone's camera spots enemy vehicles for your team (3 km with a gimbal camera, 1.5 km with an FPV one). With air support on, a drone flown alone can be a respawn's aircraft (a sortie point, from beside the pad), and losing it costs 10 tickets plus one for every $2,500 it cost.
  - **Score:** the scoreboard reads Kills, Caps, Lost and Score, the bar shows both teams' tickets round the zone chips and a capture bar while you stand in a zone, and the results say how it ended ("Faroes win on tickets (212 – 0) · Highland Ridge").
- **Practice battle (vs AI)** on the multiplayer screen: the ground battle alone on your device, against the computer. Pick the era (it says which vehicle you'll drive: your blueprint's design if it fits the money, else the era's stock tank); you and two AI allies on the north pad fight three AI vehicles from the south pad for the zones, 600 tickets a side, with the same rules, wrecks and a Respawn panel. Revert to launch puts you back on your pad for the vehicle's tickets; there is no Recover. Bring a drone bay or counter-drone kit and it becomes a drone war: the enemy fields a BMK-2D Nest, which launches FPV drones at you, and a counter-drone vehicle for the money (the ZSY-23, the Gepardo or the MA-1C).
- **Carriers:** catapult launches. Start the engine and go to full throttle, then hit **LAUNCH** (`L`): the catapult throws the plane off the bow at about 280 km/h. Land on a deck with the throttle back and the arrestor wires stop you. Helicopters start on the aft deck.
- **Seeing the others:** names float over everyone's craft in team colours (blue friends, red enemies, gold in the sandbox). Enemies only show their tag within about 6 km, less if they are stealthy, or further while your own radar part holds them (in a ground battle, only while spotted: above); tap an enemy's tag to make it your target. With a warning receiver (RWR) on board, a radar lock on you flashes RADAR LOCK, and the Advanced RWR's scope shows where it comes from and which radars are sweeping you; a radar switched off (`O`) is silent on everyone's warning receiver. A drone shows only when you can see it: 400 m by eye, or further with a drone detector or a Drone Radar aboard (no aircraft radar sees one).
- **Chat and score:** `T` or `Enter` chats in flight, the lobby has a chat box, a kill feed runs down the side, and the scoreboard (hold `` ` ``, or the Score button) shows kills, deaths and damage.

## What's in it

- **The real Earth:** real mountains, sea floors, coastlines and colours on the 3D globe, the ground you fly over and the map, from about 5 km per height sample. The real rivers carve their valleys (the Nile through Cairo, the Amazon past Manaus, the Seine through Paris) and the big lakes sit at their real levels (Lake Victoria, the Great Lakes, Lake Geneva); seaplanes can land on them and roads cross them on bridges. Real motorways and roads cross the land, levelled into the hills. About 6,500 real towns and cities stand where they are, as full-size streets of houses and blocks of flats with a downtown of towers in the big ones, farmland makes a patchwork of fields around them, forests grow where the satellite photo is green and snow lies where it is white. At night the cities light up, on the ground and seen from orbit. The HUD says where you are ("Over France · Paris"). The space centre stands on the west coast of the Faroe Islands (62°N, 7°W), green, treeless and ringed with basalt sea cliffs, with a lighthouse, wind turbines on the hills, a tank farm and a seaplane pier, and now a hamlet of grass-roofed houses, a farm, sheep walls, rocks and a few groves; on the plateau south-east of the runway, the **tank yard**: a concrete pad with eight painted bays, four garages, a workshop, a fuel depot inside sandbags, stacked containers and crates, and past it a driving course of concrete-block gates, a ring of tyres, a row of hay bales, a wall and a hedge to get round. It is solid the way Highland Ridge's props are (below), for planes, cars and their guns too, so it's a place to try a tank's gun and tracks (or a strafing run). Its runway 12/30 runs ESE across the islands, and past its end the road runs down a headland to a slipway into the sea (the old causeway to Shetland is gone: it was ugly and unrealistic); Shetland, with a port town on its west shore, lies 18 km on across open water. Sumburgh airfield sits on Shetland's southern tip and Wick airfield on the north coast of Scotland, each with its own runway, lights, hangars and tower, and three aircraft carriers ride at anchor in the sea between them, with a flight deck you can land on (the arrestor wires catch you) and catapults to launch from. The game keeps its pad on its own equator, so a launch to the east goes into an orbit inclined 62° over the real Earth, as it would from the real Faroes. Villages dot the land and fishing boats and freighters the sea.
- **A 3D world:** craft move and turn in all three dimensions: orbits can be inclined, planes bank and turn anywhere over the island, cars drive off across the countryside. You see textured planets from space, terrain with oceans, beaches, trees and clouds near the ground, the space centre with its runway, helipad and the road down the headland, shadows, engine plumes and smoke. The camera follows behind planes, cars and helicopters and beside rockets. **The ground you see is the ground you touch:** round your craft whenever it is on or near the ground (any tank or car, or anything within 150 m of the ground), the terrain is drawn from the very heights the physics stands on, so tracks and wheels sit on the hillside you see instead of sinking into a drawn hill or hovering over a drawn dip (it was 0.5-4 m off within 100 m of you; now within a few centimetres), and other vehicles nearby sit on it too. It hands over seamlessly to the far terrain and to Highland Ridge's own ground, and fades in and out as a plane comes down to it or climbs away.
- **Vehicles:** rockets launch from the pad, planes take off from the runway, cars drive from it, helicopters and drones lift off from the helipad (a winged drone, which can't hover, starts in the air over it), and Armour starts on Highland Ridge's north pad (below); in Sandbox you can pick somewhere else (Spawn points, below). Wings make lift from the airflow and stall at high angles; ailerons roll, elevators pitch and the tail fin's rudder yaws and keeps a plane pointing into the wind, so banked turns are coordinated. Propellers and jets need air, rotors give lift and the stick tilts a helicopter (it levels itself when you let go), car wheels drive and the front ones steer, landing gear rolls freely and everything brakes. A new plane flight starts on the autopilot's hold, as it always did (Settings → Planes start in can start it in level, heading, Off or Aim instead). **Aim** (on the autopilot bar) is the War Thunder-style mouse aim: you move a circle (the mouse, or a finger dragging the view) and an instructor flies the plane's nose to it as a pilot would, banking into the turn and pulling, pushing over with the wings level for a circle just under the nose, short of the stall and inside the wings' g; one cross shows where the nose points now and the guns hit (green when a gun or rocket pod is selected, pale blue otherwise), so the HUD holds just the two, as War Thunder's does, and the camera follows the circle a moment behind, quick at first and easing in as it closes (never so far that the circle leaves the middle of the screen: at most 60% of the way to its edge), over the top in a loop too. Keep the circle on a target in a hard turn and the guns stay on it within a degree. The nose (and the cross) goes right onto the circle and answers a small flick of it in under half a second; low over the ground a circle on the horizon holds your height instead, even in a low pass over the sea, and climbs over hills; a circle put on the ground is dived at, the nose (and the guns) right on it, all the way down: pulling out is yours, as in War Thunder (Settings → Aim pulls out of dives has the instructor pull out by itself at the last moment, about 25 m up; it's off by default), and it holds a dive short of the wings' speed limit; a quick jet rolls over and pulls to a circle well below it rather than pushing; on the runway it steers the take-off roll and lifts off, and with the gear down a circle on the runway is where it lands, flaring at the end. The throttle stays yours. The keys or the stick still fly it by hand, within the autopilot's limits, the circle following the nose. Or fly the stick with the autopilot: hold keeps the pitch and bank you let go at, level holds the altitude and heading a heading. - **Air-to-air missiles can be beaten** (in the spirit of War Thunder):
  - **Energy:** the motor burns for a few seconds, then the missile coasts and loses speed to drag (more in the thick air down low) and to every turn. Its fins only pull their full g while it is fast, so a long or low shot can fall short.
  - **Seekers:** a missile sees only so far off its nose and can only swing its seeker so fast. It arms after 0.6 s.
  - **Heat seekers** lock at a range set by the target's heat: a jet at full power from behind at 5 km, one at idle or coming head-on at 1 to 2 km, a propeller plane closer still. Flares (`K`) fool them, far more often when the target throttles back than in afterburner.
  - **Radar missiles** need your radar (a radar part: below) on the target the whole way. If you turn away, they go dumb, unless they are active ones that have gone on alone (MISSILE ACTIVE on the target's warning receiver). A target that dives low and flies across your beam ("notching") or drops chaff can shake them, and a jammer pod slows a radar lock and shortens a radar's reach (less so for the radars that resist jamming). Chaff from a **Chaff Dispenser** (24 bundles) is far stronger than the cloud that comes free with flares: it works best once the missile is within a few kilometres and you fly across its path. `K` (or the CM button) drops flares and a chaff bundle together. The HUD shows ✦ flares and ≋ chaff left, and with an Advanced RWR the warning reads RADAR MISSILE when a radar-guided one is after you, so you know to drop chaff or notch. The Falcon, Kestrel, Striker and Spectre carry a dispenser, and enemy fighters drop chaff against your radar missiles too.
  - **Warnings come from a part:** only a radar warning receiver (RWR, in the Weapons tab) tells you a missile is homing on you, solo and in multiplayer, heat seekers too. The **Basic RWR** ($1,000) flashes MISSILE with a warning tone (and RADAR LOCK when a radar holds you: another player's, or flying solo a bandit's with you in its nose, the frigate's guiding a SAM at you, or a computer SPAA's), but not what kind of missile or where from. The **Advanced RWR** ($3,000) adds a threat display: a small scope with every missile round your craft (IR a heat seeker, R a radar missile) and every radar lock on its rim, faint strobes for every radar sweeping you, and over the nose the nearest missile's kind, distance and seconds to impact, and what to do ("FLARES NOW · break turn", "put it on your wing and dive (notch)", "CHAFF NOW"; MISSILE ACTIVE and "ACTIVE: chaff now" for an active radar missile that has gone on alone). A quiet AESA's lock is heard only within about 10 km. Flying solo, with one on board, your countermeasures go out by themselves: the right decoy with the Advanced RWR, flares and chaff together with the basic one, which can't tell them apart. Without one nothing warns you, and nothing goes out by itself: look around, because every hostile missile in sight within 4 km has a red MSL marker over it, like the markers over other planes. The stock military planes carry one (the basic one up to the 3rd generation, the advanced one after); the warbirds don't.
- **Radar (the big update): no magic radar.** A cockpit or a nose has no radar of its own any more: you fit one. The **Radar** tab (planes and helicopters) has nine:
  - **Inside a nose** (a cockpit or a fuselage works too; a Radome has the most room): the **Ranging Radar** ($1,000: 8 km dead ahead, leads your guns out to 3 km and cues heat seekers so they lock faster; it guides nothing and can't see below you), the **Pulse Radar** ($2,500: 14 km, ±45°, guides radar missiles at one target, but can't look down: anything below you is lost in the ground's echo), the **Pulse-Doppler Radar** ($3,500: 18 km, ±60°, looks down, two targets at once, and lets active missiles go on alone; a low target flying across its beam can still hide: notching), the **PESA Radar** ($6,500: 24 km, locks in a second, four targets, shrugs off half a jammer, hard to notch) and the **AESA Radar** ($9,000: 26 km, ±70°, locks in 0.7 s, six targets, hardest to jam or notch, and quiet: warning receivers hear it only within about 10 km); and the **Tail Warning Radar** ($1,200: looks behind and calls CHECK SIX, with a tone, when an enemy aircraft is within 3 km of your tail).
  - **Outside:** the **Radar Pod** ($4,000: for a plane with no room in its nose, under a wing or the fuselage: 12 km, one target; it spoils stealth like any store), the **Mast Radar** ($6,000: a dome over a helicopter's rotor that sees all round to 9 km, picks out low, slow targets other radars lose and marks vehicles on the ground out to 8 km) and the **Radar Rotodome** ($12,000: all round to 45 km, four targets, slow to lock).
  - **Fitting one:** tap a set in the tray and it slides into the front of the nose (a tail set into the back), the nose goes see-through for a moment so you see it there, and a tap on another set swaps them; or drag it into the body you want (it shows red where there's no room).   - **Speed:** push a wing past its speed and it flutters: the view shakes, the wind roars, and within a few seconds (at once, far past it) it tears off. ## Scale

Like Spaceflight Simulator, the solar system is shrunk. Earth has a 315 km radius, real surface gravity (9.8 m/s²) and a 30 km atmosphere; low orbit takes about 2.7 km/s of Δv. The Moon orbits 6,200 km out. Earth circles the Sun at 200,000 km in about 6.5 days, so a trip to Mars takes about four and a half days of game time, a few seconds at full warp.

Scripts are plain (non-module) JavaScript so the game also works when opened straight from disk.

The Earth map is baked from open data: elevation and sea-floor depth from the [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) (Mapzen terrarium, built from SRTM, GMTED and ETOPO1), land, lakes, rivers, roads, urban areas, towns, countries and seas from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) 1:10m, and NASA's Blue Marble (colour) and Black Marble (night lights). `python3 tools/bake-earth.py <folder>` rebuilds `assets/` from them in about a minute; the script lists where to get each file. The baked map is about 4.5 MB (the game caches it for offline play).

The game started as Orbit Forge, a 2D rocket game, and was then called Rocket Science Simulator. It was renamed Overbuilt once it had grown well beyond rockets: the old name survives only in internal identifiers such as the `rss.` save keys and the `RSS1.`/`RSS2.`/`RSS3.` design codes, which stay so that saves and shared codes keep working. Before that, this repository held Cam-Strike, a 5v5 FPS. Its full history is still in git: the last version is commit `30e7bc8`.
