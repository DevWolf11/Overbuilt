# Rocket Science Simulator

Build any machine from parts, then take it anywhere and do anything with it. The game mixes the rocketry of *Spaceflight Simulator* and *Kerbal Space Program*, the free-form aircraft building of *SimplePlanes*, and the combat of *War Thunder*. It is free, and it runs in a browser on a PC, a phone or a tablet, with nothing to install.

You attach parts together on a blueprint, in 2D or in 3D: rockets, planes, helicopters and cars, with engines, wings, weapons and paint wherever you want them. Then you fly in a full 3D world. Earth is the real one, shrunk to 1/20 scale, and the space centre sits on the Faroe Islands. From there you can:
- launch from the pad, reach orbit and explore a small solar system: land on the Moon, drive a rover, parachute onto Mars, dive past the Sun, and bring the crew home through a fiery reentry;
- fly planes that bank and turn over the islands and break the sound barrier, drive the causeway across the sea to Shetland, or hover a helicopter;
- fight AI targets and fighters, or other players online, in a free-for-all sandbox or in battles where everyone builds on a budget and then fights.

It runs in any modern browser on desktop and mobile. There is no build step: open `index.html` and play. The 3D world uses three.js, which ships in the repo. On a device without WebGL the game falls back to a flat 2D side view.

## Play

- **Online:** https://devwolf11.github.io/Rocket-Science-Simulator/ (deployed from `main` by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`). On a phone, use "Add to Home Screen" to install it and play offline.
- **Locally:** serve the folder (`npx serve .`). Offline play and installing need the HTTPS site above, because the service worker only registers over `https:`. Double-clicking `index.html` works too, but browsers don't let a page opened from disk read the Earth map, so Earth falls back to generated terrain.
- **Single file:** `node tools/build-single.mjs` writes `dist/rocket-science-simulator.html`, the whole game in one HTML file you can send to someone.

## Modes

- **Career:** start with $50,000 and basic parts. Every launch costs the price of its parts; recovering a craft on Earth refunds 90%. Complete 41 missions (first hop, orbit, flybys, orbits, landings and returns for every body, a Sun dive, interstellar escape, plus a first takeoff, a runway landing, the sound barrier, Mach 2, a seaplane round trip, a hover and a road trip) to earn funds, and spend them in R&D to unlock five tiers of technology.
- **Sandbox:** every part unlocked and no money involved.
- **Multiplayer:** play with friends in your browsers, with no server and no accounts. See [Multiplayer](#multiplayer).

## Multiplayer

Press **Multiplayer** on the home screen, type a name, and either create a room or join one with its five-letter code. **Online** connects browsers directly to each other (WebRTC, through the free PeerJS service to find each other); **This device** joins other tabs of the same browser, which is handy for trying it alone or playing offline. The player who creates the room hosts it: their browser runs the match, and the room closes when they leave.

- **Sandbox** (up to 5 players): the normal world, every part, no rules. Build anything in the hangar and fly it into the shared sky; everyone sees everyone's craft, smoke and gunfire, and weapons work on each other. Crash, and you fly again or go back to the hangar. Everyone shares the host's clock, so the sun, the clouds and the time of day agree.
- **Battle** (1v1, 2v2, 3v3 or a six-player free-for-all): the host picks the format, the money ($45k, $75k or $120k), the build time (2, 3 or 5 minutes), whether there is a test flight, carriers, friendly fire, time of day and a time limit (10, 15 or 25 minutes). Then:
  1. **Build.** Everyone builds against the clock with the money they have; the stats show the cost against the budget. Battles are for planes, helicopters and cars. First pick a spawn on the map (War Thunder style): team Faroes starts on the space centre's runway or Carrier West, team Shetland at Sumburgh airfield on the southern tip of Shetland or Carrier East, and a free-for-all adds Wick airfield on the north coast of Scotland and Carrier South. A carrier gives 30% more money, but the craft has to fit the deck and the catapult: at most 13 m across, 16 m long and 12 tonnes, planes and helicopters only. Press Ready when you're done; the phase ends early once everyone is ready.
  2. **Test flight** (optional, one minute): fly your design alone, with nobody to shoot you, then get **two minutes to polish it**.
  3. **Battle.** Everyone spawns at once. One life each: the last team (or pilot) flying wins, and when the time runs out the side with the most craft still up wins, then the most kills. Shot down? Watch your teammates (`[` / `]`). A design that is over budget or breaks the carrier limits when the battle starts is swapped for a stock plane that fits.
- **Carriers:** catapult launches. Start the engine and go to full throttle, then hit **LAUNCH** (`L`): the catapult throws the plane off the bow at about 280 km/h. Land on a deck with the throttle back and the arrestor wires stop you. Helicopters start on the aft deck.
- **Seeing the others:** names float over everyone's craft in team colours (blue friends, red enemies, gold in the sandbox). Enemies only show their tag within about 6 km, less if they are stealthy. A radar lock on you shows as a red ring on the HUD pointing at whoever has you.
- **Chat and score:** `T` or `Enter` chats in flight, the lobby has a chat box, a kill feed runs down the side, and the scoreboard (hold `` ` ``, or the Score button) shows kills, deaths and damage.

## What's in it

- **The real Earth:** real mountains, sea floors, coastlines and colours on the 3D globe, the ground you fly over and the map, from about 5 km per height sample. The real rivers carve their valleys (the Nile through Cairo, the Amazon past Manaus, the Seine through Paris) and the big lakes sit at their real levels (Lake Victoria, the Great Lakes, Lake Geneva); seaplanes can land on them and roads cross them on bridges. Real motorways and roads cross the land, levelled into the hills. About 6,500 real towns and cities stand where they are, as full-size streets of houses and blocks of flats with a downtown of towers in the big ones, farmland makes a patchwork of fields around them, forests grow where the satellite photo is green and snow lies where it is white. At night the cities light up, on the ground and seen from orbit. The HUD says where you are ("Over France · Paris"). The space centre stands on the west coast of the Faroe Islands (62°N, 7°W), green, treeless and ringed with basalt sea cliffs, with a lighthouse, wind turbines on the hills, a tank farm and a seaplane pier. Its runway 12/30 runs ESE across the islands, and the causeway carries on 18 km over the sea to a port town on Shetland. Sumburgh airfield sits on Shetland's southern tip and Wick airfield on the north coast of Scotland, each with its own runway, lights, hangars and tower, and three aircraft carriers ride at anchor in the sea between them, with a flight deck you can land on (the arrestor wires catch you) and catapults to launch from. The game keeps its pad on its own equator, so a launch to the east goes into an orbit inclined 62° over the real Earth, as it would from the real Faroes. Villages dot the land and fishing boats and freighters the sea.
- **A 3D world:** craft move and turn in all three dimensions: orbits can be inclined, planes bank and turn anywhere over the island, cars drive off across the countryside. You see textured planets from space, terrain with oceans, beaches, trees and clouds near the ground, the space centre with its runway, helipad and causeway, shadows, engine plumes and smoke. The camera follows behind planes, cars and helicopters and beside rockets.
- **Vehicles:** rockets launch from the pad, planes take off from the runway, cars drive from it (a causeway road leads 18 km across the sea to Shetland), and helicopters lift off from the helipad. Wings make lift from the airflow and stall at high angles; ailerons roll, elevators pitch and the tail fin's rudder yaws and keeps a plane pointing into the wind, so banked turns are coordinated. Propellers and jets need air, rotors give lift and the stick tilts a helicopter (it levels itself when you let go), car wheels drive and the front ones steer, landing gear rolls freely and everything brakes. Planes hold their pitch and bank when you let go, or the autopilot can level them out or hold a heading. In every autopilot mode the rudder keeps turns coordinated, so the plane doesn't skid or fishtail. The ailerons roll more gently when the plane is slow or already sliding sideways, and the nose comes down before a climb runs out of speed. Off is the bare stick, with none of that help.
- **Weapons and combat:** machine guns, a 30 mm autocannon, a seven-barrel rotary cannon and gun pods; rocket pods; heat-seeking missiles, radar missiles (they reach far only with a radar in the nose), attack missiles, anti-radar missiles that home on radars, SAM sites, AA guns and frigates, and a cruise missile that flies itself to a target 30 km away; 250 kg bombs, a 1-tonne bomb, an internal bomb bay, cluster bombs that burst into 24 bomblets, guided glide bombs and torpedoes that swim to boats and ships; flares and chaff, an ECM jammer pod (incoming SAMs lose you, AA gunners aim badly, radar locks take longer) and a defensive turret that turns and fires by itself at fighters and drones. Guns fire tracers (hold `V`), missiles lock on to what's in front of the nose (a growl, then a steady tone and LOCK), bombs fall where the bombsight on the HUD shows, and the gun pipper and a lead marker help you aim. Stores leave their pylons as they go, and the craft gets lighter.
- **Air-to-air missiles can be beaten** (in the spirit of War Thunder):
  - **Energy:** the motor burns for a few seconds, then the missile coasts and loses speed to drag (more in the thick air down low) and to every turn. Its fins only pull their full g while it is fast, so a long or low shot can fall short.
  - **Seekers:** a missile sees only so far off its nose and can only swing its seeker so fast. It arms after 0.6 s.
  - **Heat seekers** lock at a range set by the target's heat: a jet at full power from behind at 5 km, one at idle or coming head-on at 1 to 2 km, a propeller plane closer still. Flares (`K`) fool them, far more often when the target throttles back than in afterburner.
  - **Radar missiles** need your nose radar on the target the whole way. If you turn away, they go dumb. A target that dives low and flies across your beam ("notching") or drops chaff can shake them, and a jammer pod slows a radar lock.
  - **Warnings:** radar missiles set off the target's warning; in multiplayer a heat seeker comes in silently unless the target carries a jammer, which doubles as a missile warner.
- **Targets around the islands:** tanks, trucks, radars, bunkers, AA guns and SAM sites on the Faroes and Shetland, patrol boats and a frigate at sea and target drones in the air, each bracketed on the HUD with its range. Settings › Targets: Practice (they just take it), Hostile (AA guns and the frigate fire back, SAM sites launch heat seekers that flares can fool, and enemy fighters come hunting, flying the same physics as you) or Off. Destroyed targets come back elsewhere a minute later.
- **Damage:** every part has health. Bullets, shells, rockets and explosions wear it down; a damaged engine loses thrust and smokes, then burns, a damaged wing loses lift, holed tanks leak, and a part at zero breaks off or blows up (fuel and ammunition go up with it). A hard landing dents parts instead of wrecking them; only a real crash breaks them outright. The HUD shows how much of the craft is damaged. An armoured fuselage turns away 60% of the damage.
- **Stealth:** stealth noses, fuselages and the internal bomb bay hide a plane from radar: the more of its hull is stealthy, the closer enemy SAMs, AA guns and fighters have to be to find it (the weapon box shows it as ◈). Bombs, missiles, pods and tanks hung outside spoil it.
- **Structure:** every wing has a load limit, and every design a never-exceed speed. Sturdy fighter wings take 8 g, a glider's give way at a fraction of that, and an airliner sits in between. The HUD shows the g-load against what the wings can take, and a warning (with a beep) comes up before they give. A moment over the limit is survivable: stress builds up while you hold it and fades once you ease off, so a hard pull in a sturdy plane is fine and only a held over-g (or a far-over one) bends the wings and tears them off. Past the never-exceed speed you get an overspeed warning, but nothing breaks. The builder shows each design's g-limit and never-exceed speed.
- **The sound barrier:** past Mach 1 in thick air a vapour cone wraps the craft, a shock ring bursts outwards and a sonic boom cracks; the HUD shows the Mach number.
- **157 parts** in fourteen categories: crew pods and probe cores; tanks in 1, 2 and 3 m widths; nine rocket engines including solid boosters that can't be shut down, an aerospike that keeps its efficiency at every altitude, a nuclear engine and an ion drive; separators; nose cones, fins and heat shields; parachutes, landing legs, rover wheels and RCS; girders and adapters; ground parts (driver cab, chassis, car wheels); 94 aircraft parts and 19 weapons:
  - **Cockpits:** bush, standard, fighter-nose and airliner cockpits, a two-seat tandem trainer canopy, a faceted stealth nose with a gold canopy, a glazed bomber nose, a warbird cockpit section, an all-glass helicopter bubble and a pilotless drone core.
  - **Fuselage:** a radome, plain, long, slim and wide fuselages, a cargo hull with a loading ramp, a fuel-tank fuselage, an armoured tub, a faceted stealth body, a fuselage with cheek intakes, a slim tail boom for helicopters and twin-boom planes, two adapters, four tail cones and a spinning radar rotodome that triples how far the HUD sees targets.
  - **Wings:** short, standard and long wings (with flaps and fuel), swept, delta and glider wings, a swing wing that folds back by itself as you speed up, a forward-swept wing, an elliptical wing, braced biplane wings, a long straight transport wing, an airliner wing with winglets, strakes for flying at wild angles, two tailplanes, two elevators, a canard, a V-tail that pitches and yaws at once, four tail fins including a twin-tail pair, and air brakes.
  - **Aero engines:** small, standard and four-blade propellers, a V12 behind a pointed spinner, contra-rotating props (no torque roll), a ducted fan, an electric motor with its own battery, a turboprop, a pusher prop, twin wing-mounted props, a pulsejet, small and standard turbojets, an afterburning jet, a fighter turbofan, a thrust-vectoring jet, jet pods, standard and geared turbofans and fan pods of each, a ramjet that only lights at speed, a scramjet for Mach 3 to 7, a rocket motor for X-planes, JATO bottles and helicopter rotors.
  - **Gear:** small, standard and heavy gear, small, standard and heavy retractable gear, a four-wheel airliner bogie, fat tundra tyres, a tail wheel, helicopter skids, floats, drop tanks, a brake chute and an air-show smoke generator.
  - **Weapons:** machine gun, autocannon, rotary cannon, gun pods, a gun turret, rocket pods, heat-seeking, radar, attack, anti-radar and cruise missiles, bombs, a heavy bomb, an internal bomb bay, cluster bombs, glide bombs, torpedoes, flares and an ECM jammer.
- **Flight model:** wings and tail fins mount straight through the fuselage, and pods, floats and drop tanks hang off them in mirrored pairs (engine pods fixed to the fuselage instead get pylons). Each wing gets its own lift slope from its aspect ratio and sweep, induced drag (less for an elliptical wing or winglets, more for a biplane, which also drags its struts and wires), a stall angle, dihedral that rolls you level, ground effect and transonic wave drag; a swing wing's sweep, lift and wave drag change with the Mach number; flaps add lift and drag, control surfaces stiffen at high speed, and pulling too many g tears wings and tails off. Jets gain thrust with ram air and afterburners kick in above 90% throttle, turbofans lose thrust as they speed up, single props twist the plane (contra-rotating ones don't), props break if they strike the ground, retracted gear stops dragging and seaplanes take off from the bay.
- **Builder:** build on the 2D blueprint or in 3D (mouse or touch). Parts attach to whatever they're dropped on, exactly where they're dropped: under a wing, on a fuselage's flank or back, or on the end of another part. Whatever hangs on a part moves, turns and goes with it (move a wing and its engines and missiles come along; delete it and they go too). Every part has a depth across the craft, so engines, pods, missiles and bombs can go anywhere along a wing, as many as fit: in 3D just drop them there; on the 2D side view, a part dropped onto a wing finds the first free spot along it, Duplicate puts another one further out, and `[` `]` or the Depth slider (Spread, for a pair) move the selected part in and out. Parts behind one another show a ×count in the side view, and a tap on the selected one picks the one behind. A Grid button (`G`, 2D only) switches the old quarter-metre grid back on. Paint: every part can wear one of thirteen paints (gloss white, greys, stealth grey, night black, navy, blue, olive drab, desert tan, red, orange, yellow, bare metal) or its own colours; Match (`M`) paints a part like the one it hangs on, All paints the whole craft like the selected part, and a new part takes the paint of what it's put on, so a wing on a stealth fuselage comes in stealth grey. Paint is only looks: radar stealth still comes from the stealth parts. Quarter-turn part rotation, symmetry (mirrored parts turn the other way; for planes, whatever goes out on one wing gets a twin on the other), automatic staging with per-stage Δv and TWR, centre-of-mass and thrust markers, cost and tech-tier locks in career, undo/redo. Shift-click (or Select mode, for touch) picks several parts, or drag a box round them; drag any of them to move them together, and copy, cut and paste them (`Ctrl+C` / `Ctrl+X` / `Ctrl+V`, also into another design), turn them, nudge them or delete them as a group. `Ctrl+A` selects everything. The Save button by the design's name keeps it in the hangar (`Ctrl+S`; tap the name to save a copy under a new name), with a dot when there are unsaved changes; the hangar lists your designs with thumbnails, warns before replacing one with the same name, and asks before loading over unsaved work. Any design can be copied as a short text code and pasted into any copy of the game.
- **Physics:** 3D rigid bodies with a full inertia tensor and quaternion attitude, gimbal and reaction wheels, pressure-dependent Isp, drag and fin stability, wings simulated in halves so rolling damps itself, reentry heating that burns away the leading part unless a heat shield takes it, parachutes limited by dynamic pressure (drogue first, main canopy near the ground), oceans you can splash down in, rover driving with steering, self-levelling and a hand back onto the wheels if it tips over, and per-corner ground contact on the 3D terrain where hard landings break parts. A rocket's legs and fins stand in fours around the stack.
- **Solar system:** the Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth and the Moon, Mars and Phobos, Jupiter and Europa, each with its own gravity, terrain, colours, and atmosphere where it has one. Patched conics with spheres of influence; time warp up to 1,000,000× runs craft on exact Kepler orbits and stops automatically at atmospheres and sphere changes.
- **Navigation:** the map opens on a 3D globe of the planet you're at, north up, with a grid of real latitude and longitude, countries, seas and cities, the space centre, the track you've flown and where you're heading (a great circle with a tick for every minute at your speed) or the orbit you're on, drawn in 3D over the ground. Drag to turn it, pinch or scroll to zoom from the whole planet down to a few kilometres, where it paints the ground as the game shapes it; a strip shows your latitude and longitude and the distance and bearing home. The HUD shows a compass heading for planes, cars and helicopters. `N` (or the System button) switches to the solar-system map, which predicts up to four patches ahead, showing apoapsis and periapsis, encounters (with the periapsis you'll get), escapes and impacts. Tap a planet to target it and see the closest approach and when the next transfer window opens. Autopilot modes hold prograde, retrograde, radial or target, and prograde/retrograde markers sit around the craft. A Δv map covers every destination.
- **Graphics:** day and night as Earth circles the Sun, sunsets and sun glare, stars and a faint Milky Way, oceans with surf and beaches, lit cumulus clouds you can fly through, trees, rocks, villages with lit windows at night, a launch site with a tower and buildings, shaded planets with atmospheres, Mars' ice caps and Jupiter's bands, engine plumes per propellant with shock diamonds, smoke, dust kicked off the ground, reentry plasma, heat glow on parts, explosions with flashes and shockwaves.
- **Saving:** designs and flights save automatically (also when a phone backgrounds the page). Resume a flight from the home screen.
- **Settings:** volume, graphics quality (shadows, scenery, effects), flight hints, markers, fullscreen, career reset. The 3D view lowers its resolution on its own when frames run long and raises it again when there is time to spare.

## Scale

Like Spaceflight Simulator, the solar system is shrunk. Earth has a 315 km radius, real surface gravity (9.8 m/s²) and a 30 km atmosphere; low orbit takes about 2.7 km/s of Δv. The Moon orbits 6,200 km out. Earth circles the Sun at 200,000 km in about 6.5 days, so a trip to Mars takes about four and a half days of game time, a few seconds at full warp.

Scripts are plain (non-module) JavaScript so the game also works when opened straight from disk.

The Earth map is baked from open data: elevation and sea-floor depth from the [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/) (Mapzen terrarium, built from SRTM, GMTED and ETOPO1), land, lakes, rivers, roads, urban areas, towns, countries and seas from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) 1:10m, and NASA's Blue Marble (colour) and Black Marble (night lights). `python3 tools/bake-earth.py <folder>` rebuilds `assets/` from them in about a minute; the script lists where to get each file. The baked map is about 4.5 MB (the game caches it for offline play).

This repository used to hold Cam-Strike, a 5v5 FPS. Its full history is still in git: the last version is commit `30e7bc8`.
