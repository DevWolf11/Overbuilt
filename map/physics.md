# Vehicle physics on the ground (js/vessel.js ground contact, driving, steering, attitude on wheels, mass/inertia, splitting; js/flight.js stepping)

A ground vehicle today is an ordinary rigid-body Vessel with kind 'car'. Its drive frame is DRIVE.other: nose +x, up +y, right +z. No part ever moves relative to the hull for mass, inertia or contact purposes. Swing wings, nozzles, retractable gear and the Gun Turret's aim are per-part state numbers that the aero code, the renderer and combat read.

The ground is a per-step impulse solver in Vessel.ground():
- **Probes.** Every hull box corner and every wheel rim within 2+0.3·reach m of the ground under the COM costs a Terrain query. Contacts are rigid: there is no suspension.
- **Solver.** Four iterations: a normal impulse, then friction clamped to mu·jn of that same iteration (rolling mu1, sideways mu2), then a 0.9 push-out.
- **Steering.** Wheels more than 0.3 m ahead of the COM have their axle turned by `steer = ctl.h·min(0.6, 6·wheelbase/vF²)`. Yaw comes only from tyre friction, so nothing turns at a standstill.
- **Driving.** Not a wheel force. A centre-of-mass speed controller adds `clamp((ctl.v·top − vt)·1.5, ±acc)·grip·dt` along the ground. It has no torque and no traction limit, and it doesn't depend on mass (the buggy with 20 t of ballast reaches 30 m/s sooner: 7.0 s against 8.3 s).
- **Water.** The sea and lakes are a solid surface that damps velocity by e^(−0.6dt). Driving still works there, so the buggy crosses the sea at about 6.7 m/s.
- **Attitude.** On wheels, the stick never rotates the craft. A 'rover' autopilot levels it to the ground normal using reaction-wheel authority (halved on wheels), plus a strong 'righting' torque when it is on its side and slow.

The Dune Buggy (1,260 kg, COM 1.82 m above the contact plane, track 2 m) has a static rollover threshold of 0.55 g. The steering limit allows about 0.61 g, so full steer held at 9–13 m/s flips it in 2.5–3 s.

Cost: ground() is 85–97% of a step. The buggy takes about 250–340 µs a step on the ground against 38 µs in the air; a parked FS-15 (168 hull points, 164 surfaceAt calls a step) takes 870–1,020 µs. Cost grows by about 8 probes per box part.

Results hardly depend on step size: speed after 10 s is 34.86 m/s at h=1/120 and 35.00 at 1/60.

## Key facts
- Kind 'car': site 'runway', launchRot 0, builder tabs ground/utility/structure/weapons. Flight.start sets kind before rebuildGroups and places it facing east (part +x = east, +y up, +z = surfaceFrame north = game south). (js/parts.js:28; js/flight.js:42-53; js/vessel.js:1069-1081)
- PILOT.car: F +x, U +y, stick map [pitch, roll, yaw], rates 0.9/1.2/1.2. DRIVE.other (every kind but plane): F +x, U +y, R = F×U = +z. `drive` getter picks DRIVE.plane only for planes. (js/vessel.js:99-106,151-152)
- rebuildGroups has nothing car-specific. It sets wheels = parts with def.wheel and aircraft = wings.length>0, then recompute, computeAreas and computeHull. computeHull's radial leg/fin copies are rocket-only; wheelbase is the spread of wheel points along drive.F (minimum 1). (js/vessel.js:231-258,290-316)
- Wheel defs. carWheel: r 0.54, drive, speed 38 m/s, accel 5 m/s², track 2, mass 0.08 t, breakSpeed 16. Rover Wheel: r 0.45, speed 12, accel 2.5, track 2, breakSpeed 14. Gear parts have drive:false. Physics reads only r, drive, speed, accel, track, caster and retract. wheel.brake is never read (B brakes every wheel). bogie and fat are visual only. (js/parts.js:110,429,319-337; js/vessel.js:973,1004,1042; js/parts3d.js:995-996)
- One wheel part with track>0 is an axle. massZs splits its mass and its contact point into two wheels at partZ ± track/2. wheelGeom puts the centre at partPt(p,0,−0.05h) for shape 'wheel', or at the outer end of a gear strut. A wheel's partDepth is 0.4. (js/vessel.js:17-20; js/parts.js:877,917-921)
- Hull points: 8 box corners per copy for each non-wheel part; a ROUND3D shape gets 4 points per end instead. Wheels are not hull boxes: each massZs copy adds one wheelPt {x,y,z,r,retract}. computeAreas excludes wheels and wings; gearDrag adds 0.5·r per wheel copy. reach = max distance from the COM + 0.5. (js/vessel.js:263-322)
- Mass, COM and inertia. partKg = mass·1000 + fuel + store. Inertia is the full 3×3 tensor of box parts, each copy at its own z, inverted by blocks. Dune Buggy (measured): 1260 kg, com (−0.19, 1.62, 0), Ixx 1251, Iyy 1902 (the car's yaw axis), Izz 2025, Ixy 152 kg·m². 16 hull points, 4 wheel points at x ±1.4, y 0.34, z ±1, wheelbase 2.8, reach 3.31. (js/parts.js:836; js/vessel.js:171-221)
- Broad phase. Return clear() if r − reach > R + terrainMax + 5. Per probe, skip if the point is higher than lim0 = R + h0 + 2 + 0.3·reach, where h0 is the surface or deck height under the COM. Every remaining hull corner and unretracted wheel rim gets locate + surfaceAt. Each contact then also gets normalAt (4 more surfaceAt), deckAt ×2 and waterAt. (js/vessel.js:931-954)
- Crash check. Closing speed s = −v·n against lim = breakSpeed (default 9) × 1.8 on water. The part breaks at s > 1.6·lim when Combat is loaded (×1 in the headless traces). Between 0.75 and 1.6·lim it is dented (Combat.impact, once per 0.4 s). A spinning prop or rotor (rpm>8) shatters on contact. (js/vessel.js:9,956-969; js/combat.js:71-77)
- wheelsDown = any wheel contact. canDrive = !aircraft && any drive-wheel contact, so any wing part disables driving. The drive command is ctl.v (W/S, −1..1), not v.throttle. braking = brake && wheelsDown. (js/vessel.js:972-980)
- Steering. steer = ctl.h·min(0.6, 6·wheelbase/max(1, vF²)); planes use ctl.h+ctl.r, 0.5 and 3·wheelbase. Only wheels whose centre is more than 0.3 m ahead of the COM along D.F get their axle turned by steer. All wheels share one angle (no Ackermann, no rear steer). The yaw comes only from friction impulses: holding D at a standstill for 3 s gave 0° of heading change (measured). D turns right. (js/vessel.js:981-1001)
- Friction. mu1 (rolling): 0.8 braking; 0.04 driving or moving (|wheelSpeed|>0.5); 0.9 when it can drive, isn't driving and is slow (a parking brake); 0.025 with no drive wheel down; casters 0.02 unless braking. mu2 (sideways): 0.8, 0.15 on water, casters 0.25. Floats: mu1 0.035/0.45 and mu2 0.4/0.8 (water/land). Hull points: isotropic 0.9 (0.15 on water). (js/vessel.js:994-1004,1022-1028)
- Solver: 4 iterations. A contact with vn<0 gets jn = −vn·(1+bounce if vn<−2)/effMass; bounce is 0 unless the Silly 'bouncy' setting gives 0.75. Friction along t1 then t2 is clamped to ±mu·jn of that same iteration, not an accumulated impulse; once vn ≥ 0 a later iteration skips the contact entirely. Then a push-out of 0.9·maxPen along the deepest contact's normal. There is no suspension: wheels are rigid. (js/vessel.js:1006-1033)
- Drive is a centre-of-mass speed controller. f = the forward axis projected onto wheelContacts[0].n (one wheel's normal). top = min speed of the drive wheels touching (default 12); acc = max of their accel (default 2.5); grip = min(1, driveContacts/wheelPts.length). accel = clamp((ctl.v·top − vt)·1.5, ±acc)·grip when driving, else clamp(−vt·1.2, ±2)·grip; then v += f·accel·dt. It applies no torque, uses no fuel, ignores mass and has no traction limit. Reverse is symmetric. (js/vessel.js:1035-1047)
- Measured Dune Buggy, runway, h=1/120. 0→10/20/30 m/s in 2.5/5.2/8.3 s; 36.6 m/s after 30 s (top 38). Braking 36.5→0 in 4.4 s over 78 m (about 0.85 g). Coasting 36.6→21.4 m/s in 5 s. Reverse 31.6 m/s after 10 s. With 20 t of ballast it reaches 30 m/s in 7.0 s (faster). Full steer: 18.8°/s at 17.8 m/s (R 54 m); 9.7°/s at 35.6 m/s (R 210 m). (js/builder.js:136-139 (template); measured with test/lib/game.js)
- Rollover. The buggy's COM is 1.82 m above the contact plane and its half-track is 1.0 m, so it rolls at 0.55 g. The steer limit allows about 6 m/s² (0.61 g) in a steady turn. Holding full steer at 9.1 or 12.9 m/s rolls it past 100° in 2.5–3 s; at 5.4 or 17.8+ m/s it stays upright (measured). (js/vessel.js:984-985)
- Wheel visuals. One wheelSpeed for the whole craft; every wheel's p.spin −= vt/r·dt, frozen while braking. v.steer is written but nothing in parts3d, render3d or world reads it, so front wheels never visibly turn. (js/vessel.js:985,1048-1049; js/parts3d.js:1785)
- Water. The sea (map height <0 gives 0) and inland water above the ground are a solid surface. Any non-float contact in water damps v and av by e^(−0.6dt). The drive keeps working, so the buggy drives on the sea (6.7 m/s after 30 s, measured). There is no buoyancy and nothing sinks. (js/terrain.js:647-661; js/vessel.js:1051-1056)
- Landing and rest. contactTime += dt on every step with a contact; clear() resets it to 0 on any step without one. landed = contactTime > 0.2 && speed < 1.5. restTime grows while landed, not driving, speed <0.15, |av| <0.05 and thrust < 0.2·m; after 0.5 s velocity is zeroed and av halved each step. Flight lets non-active vessels sleep when landed with restTime > 2 and no thrust. (js/vessel.js:933,1058-1064; js/flight.js:631-635)
- Attitude on wheels. Stick input is ignored for rotation while on driving wheels. With sas 'hold' (kind not plane/heli, no wings), the rover branch targets ω = (u×n)·k (k 1.2 on wheels; 2.5 in the air within 40 m of the ground with no thrust). It leaves the yaw rate to the wheels and clamps each axis to ±1.2 rad/s. Torque need = I·(wt−wb)·0.35/dt, limited by authority A = reaction wheels + gimbal + rotor, halved on wheels (buggy cab: 4000 → 2000 N·m). 'Righting' (u·n <0.5, speed <4, groundAltitude <3) adds 14·m·max(1,width) N·m on every axis. (js/vessel.js:504-545,659-674)
- Cars get the rocket SAS bar (hold, prograde, …). Any mode but 'hold' turns off rover levelling, and sasHeading then steers the car's +y (its roof) toward that direction. (js/flight.js:228-235,987; js/vessel.js:533-554)
- Car air drag uses cdO 0.5 instead of 0.9, with the per-axis projected areas. (js/vessel.js:710-723)
- No part moves for mass, inertia or hull. A swing wing's p.sweep changes only its aero (slope, back, mcrit, wave, stall). A flat nozzle's p.deflect is for the eye. Retracted gear (p.retracted) only skips its wheel probes and its gear drag. Control surfaces (deflect, aileron), spins and chute/bay doors (deployed) are state numbers read by the renderer and combat. partGeo caches on x, y, z and rot, rot is in 90° steps, and boxes are axis-aligned, so the part model cannot express an arbitrary turret yaw. (js/vessel.js:50-57,161-169,677-681,953,361,720; js/flight.js:245-254; js/parts.js:860-867)
- The existing turret precedent: the Gun Turret keeps p.aimYaw and p.aimEl, slewed at 2.5 rad/s, aimed automatically at air targets only. It parks and holds fire whenever v.contactTime > 0, so it never fires from a car on the ground (rwr.test.js asserts this). turretFire turns the aim into the vessel frame through rot k. parts3d rotates rig.yaw and rig.pitch, and MP 'pv' rows carry the aim. Mass, inertia, hull and hit boxes all ignore the aim. No weapon applies recoil anywhere. (js/combat.js:351-394,486-495; js/parts3d.js:1804; js/mp.js:628-633,802-804; js/parts.js:395-399)
- Ground classification for weapons: air seekers skip ghosts with kind 'car'; ground seekers take ghosts that are landed or kind 'car'. Wheel hit boxes span track/2 + 0.3 across. (js/combat.js:455-465,82-85)
- Splitting. breakParts and ripParts remove parts and cut adjacency, then splitComponents keeps the component with the control part (the cab). Each other piece becomes a new Vessel with kind, q, av, pointVel and gearUp; isDebris = !control; then rebuildGroups. Losing a wheel recomputes wheels, wheelPts, wheelbase and grip, and the car keeps driving on what's left; exposed chassis corners scrape at mu 0.9. (js/vessel.js:1120-1164; js/flight.js:258-282,372-389)
- Step cost (Node, this container, real Earth map). Dune Buggy driving on the runway: 245–340 µs a step, against 38 µs in the air. A parked FS-15 (168 hull points): 870–1,020 µs a step, of which ground() is about 770 µs, against 20 µs in the air. That is 164 surfaceAt + 164 locate calls a step. One surfaceAt costs about 18 µs on the track with a new angle (earthRaw0), about 10 µs off the track (2 m lattice) and about 3.7 µs on a memo hit. At 120 steps/s the parked jet uses about 12% of a desktop core. (js/vessel.js:931-954; js/terrain.js:603-661)
- Frame-rate independence. App caps dt at 0.1 and Flight.update at 0.05 (a slower frame runs in slow motion). Substeps: n = max(1, ceil(total/PHYS_DT − 0.05)), h = total/n, PHYS_DT 1/120 (1/60 at 4× warp and up; multiplayer forces 1×). Measured buggy at h = 1/120, 1/108, 1/90, 1/60: speed after 10 s 34.86/34.88/34.94/35.00 m/s; 2 s of full steer turns 38.3°/38.1°/38.0°/37.1°; braking stops in 2.22–2.24 s. (js/main.js:59; js/flight.js:505-533)
- Templates. Dune Buggy = chassis (0,1.25) + cab (−0.4,2.25) + two carWheel axles (±1.4,0.4). Lunar Rover is kind 'rocket' with Rover Wheels. Dragonfly is a heli on carWheels. traces.js runs every template for 12 s with a fixed stick script (W/S at 2–4 s, A/D at 4–5 and 7–8 s), so any change to ground() moves the buggy, heli and rocket-on-pad traces. (js/builder.js:35-40,136-145; test/traces.js:8-27)
- The engine sound comes only from thrust parts, so a car with no engines drives in silence. The drive1k milestone counts kind 'car' with wheelsDown. (js/flight.js:556-558,857-859)
- Vessel.serialize writes a fixed list of per-part fields (no aimYaw/aimEl, spin or steer). restore sanitises through hasOwn(PARTS), cleanZ, cleanSkin and cleanAtt. (js/vessel.js:1166-1200)

## Extension points
- Track parts (tank and IFV running gear) @ js/parts.js wheel defs (:110, :429); massZs js/vessel.js:17-20; computeHull wheelPts js/vessel.js:297-301: Add new part keys; don't change carWheel. A track is one part (an axle with a wide track, or a twinZ pair) that adds N road-wheel contact points along its length per side, each tagged with its side from sign(z − com.z). The probe and solver stay as they are. N probes per side are far cheaper than hull probes.
- Skid steering and pivot turns @ js/vessel.js:994-1004 (per-contact mu, t1/t2) and 1035-1047 (drive): For contacts flagged as track, skip the front-wheel axle turn and set a target rolling speed per contact: v_t = ctl.v·top + side·ctl.h·ω_max·halfTrack (ctl.v = 0 gives a pivot, the two sides counter-rotating). In the solver loop, apply an impulse along k.t1 of (v_t − vt)/effMass clamped to ±mu_traction·jn, instead of the COM speed controller. Sideways mu2 (about 0.5–0.7) then gives the skid-steer turning resistance by itself: track length against width. Gate all of it on the new flag, so old wheels keep the exact old arithmetic.
- Engine power and power-to-weight @ js/vessel.js:1040-1046; js/flight.js:556-558 (sound): A new ground-engine part (def.power, kW) limits accel to min(acc, P/(m·max(v,2))) and sets top speed, with a slower reverse. Only for craft with the new parts. It also gives Sound.engine a source when there is no thrust.
- Suspension @ js/vessel.js probe (:939-951) and solver (:1006-1029): For new wheel or track contacts only: a spring and damper per contact, F = max(0, k·pen − c·vn), applied as the impulse F·dt in place of the rigid normal impulse. Per-wheel travel state on the part (p.susp) for the renderer. Keep the rigid path for existing defs.
- Turret: aim as part state (the current precedent) @ js/combat.js:351-394 (turretStep, turretFire); js/parts3d.js:1804; js/mp.js:628-633,802-804: Keep p.aimYaw and p.aimEl on a turret part, slewed at a per-def traverse and elevation rate. Add a new weapon kind (for example a cannon turret) that does not park on the ground and takes its aim from the player's view. Physics keeps the build pose for mass, inertia and hull; the error is second order for a turret centred on its ring. For player-built turrets, a 'turret ring' part whose att subtree gets a render-time yaw in parts3d and a rotated hit box in Combat.boxesOf. Don't move p.x, p.y or p.z per step (that breaks the partGeo, hull and areas caches).
- Recoil and hit impulses @ js/vessel.js:988-992 (the impulse closure inside ground): Lift the same impulse arithmetic into a Vessel method, applyImpulse(rPart, dirWorld, j): v += dir·j/m and av += I⁻¹(r×dir)·j. Call it from Combat on firing (and on a heavy hit) for the new gun kinds only.
- Cheaper contacts for ground vehicles @ js/vessel.js:934-954 (lim0 cull); computeHull :290-323: For the new kind or flag only: precompute in computeHull a 'bottom' subset of hull points (the lowest corners along D.U), or test points first against a local plane (h0 and one normalAt, already near :935) and probe only those within about 0.5 m of it. Today the margin of 2 + 0.3·reach sends almost every corner of a low vehicle to the terrain.
- Vehicle kind @ js/parts.js:25-30 (VEHICLE_KINDS); js/vessel.js:99-106,152,711; js/flight.js:228-235,857,987,1264,1420; js/combat.js:459,462; js/mp.js:539,701,717: Either add a kind (for example 'tank') with PILOT and DRIVE frames (launchRot 0, F +x, U +y), updating every kind==='car' check and the multiplayer kind defaults; or keep kind 'car' and make tracks and turrets part features, which touches fewer places. The ground vehicle's SAS bar needs its own set (today it is the rocket's).
- Fording and sinking in water @ js/vessel.js:1051-1056: Gated on the new parts or kind: no drive (or slow sinking) while a non-float contact is in water deeper than a fording depth; amphibious IFVs keep driving with a float-like flag.
- Per-wheel and per-track visual state @ js/vessel.js:985,1048-1049; js/parts3d.js:1785-1821; js/mp.js:628: Write p.steer on steered wheels (v.steer exists unread) and a per-side spin for treads. Add tracks and turrets to the multiplayer 'pv' row filter (it only covers wings, thrust, weapons, chutes and retractable wheels).
- Save and restore of the new state @ js/vessel.js:1166-1200: Add turret aim and any per-part damage state to serialize, and sanitise it in restore (finite numbers, clamped).

## Constraints
- Existing designs must step bit-for-bit as before. test/traces.js runs the Dune Buggy, the Dragonfly and Hummingbird (helis on the ground), and every rocket on the pad through ground(). Gate new contact, drive and steer behaviour on new def fields or a new kind; never retune the constants at vessel.js:994-1047 for existing wheel defs.
- Never change carWheel, wheel, chassis or cab keys, w/h, twinZ or z-lock. Add new parts for tracks, hulls and turrets.
- Set kind before rebuildGroups(), and call rebuildGroups() after any change to parts or adj (the hull, wheelPts and wheelbase depend on kind).
- Don't move parts' x, y, z or rot per step for a turret. partGeo is keyed on them (recompute redoes the geometry), and the hull and areas are only rebuilt in rebuildGroups. Rotation in the part model is 90° steps only.
- Physics stays frame-rate independent: forces scale with dt and per-step impulses assume h ≈ 1/120 (1/60 at ≥4× warp; multiplayer forces 1×). Don't add per-frame physics.
- Battery: ground() is 85–97% of a ground step and scales with the probe count. Measure CPU before and after (an 8-corner box part adds about 8 terrain queries a step), and avoid per-step allocation in new code.
- Existing turrets must keep parking on the ground (PR #37; rwr.test.js checks it). New ground-firing behaviour needs a new weapon kind or flag.
- Multiplayer: ghosts are never stepped; visual part state travels in 'pv' rows. Bump NET_VERSION if an older peer would build, pose or hit a vehicle differently. Damage to others goes through MP.sendHit. Peer rows are sanitised in mpSpawnRows.
- 2D and 3D must agree for any new tread or turret drawing (parts.js 2D art against parts3d models).
- Control comes first: prefer warnings (rollover, overload) to taking control away. The auto-righting is part of the fun.
- Player-visible names follow the altered-name rule (no real designations).
- Shared return values: qrot without an output allocates, but Terrain.locate(…, loc) fills a shared object, and surfaceSpot() returns _loc. Copy them before the next call.

## Risks
- The drive ignores mass and traction. A 60 t tank would accelerate like the 1.26 t buggy (heavier is even faster: less relative drag), climb about 30° slopes regardless of grip, and keep driving with one wheel down (scaled by grip). War Thunder's feel needs power-to-weight and traction from the normal load.
- Rollover already happens: the buggy flips under sustained full steer at 9–13 m/s (0.55 g threshold against the steer limit's 0.61 g). Tall builds (SPAA radars, missile racks) will flip more easily; righting only helps below 4 m/s.
- Probe cost. A 30-part box-built tank is about 240 hull probes a step, roughly 1–2 ms on desktop Node: 12–24% of a core at 120 Hz, several times that on a phone. Every vehicle within NEAR_DIST 6 km is stepped, and only still, landed non-active vessels sleep.
- canDrive is false with any wing part, so a vehicle with a spoiler or a launcher built from wing parts silently can't drive and gets the plane autopilot.
- Water is solid: tanks will drive across the sea at about 6–8 m/s and ride on lakes. Fixing it for all cars changes the buggy's traces.
- Friction is clamped per iteration with no accumulated impulse, and a contact is skipped once vn ≥ 0. A per-contact track drive in the same loop will get traction that depends on which iteration resolves vn; switching to accumulated impulses would change every old ground trace.
- The drive direction comes from wheelContacts[0].n, a single wheel's normal. On the 2 m bilinear terrain lattice, a long tank spanning 3–4 cells sees normals jump at cell edges.
- Turret hit boxes, muzzles and blasts use the build pose (Combat.boxesOf), so a turned turret would be hit where it was built unless combat learns the aim.
- No suspension: rigid contacts with 0.9 push-out jolt a vehicle at speed over rough off-track terrain.
- The front/rear split depends on the COM (>0.3 m ahead). On a multi-axle vehicle the steered set changes as the COM moves (fuel, stores), and wheels within 0.3 m of the COM never steer.
- def.wheel.brake is unused, so every wheel brakes. A design that relies on unbraked wheels doesn't exist yet but could surprise.
- Cars use the rocket SAS bar. Pressing a direction mode (prograde etc.) pitches the car's roof toward it, fighting the wheels.

## Open questions
- A new kind ('tank' or 'ground') with its own frames, SAS bar, site and weapon classification, or kind 'car' with track and turret parts (fewer touchpoints: vessel.js:711, flight.js:857/1264/1420, combat.js:459/462, mp.js kind defaults)?
- Should old car wheels keep the mass-independent speed controller exactly (the invariant says yes), with power and traction only for new running gear?
- Suspension: worth adding for new running gear (feel, battery cost about the same per contact), or keep rigid contacts and rely on the push-out?
- Turrets: a catalogue of single-part turrets (cheap, fits the current aim-state precedent), or player-built turrets on a ring part (render-time transforms of att subtrees, rotated hit boxes, builder clash rules in 3D)?
- Should non-amphibious ground vehicles stop or sink in deep water? Changing this for the existing buggy moves its baseline.
- How is the turret aimed on touch screens: camera direction, a second stick? (Camera and HUD area.) Does the hull's stick then drive, with A/D skid-steering?
- Should a ground vehicle's loss be the control part (the cab), as today, or a crew or ammo model like War Thunder? That changes splitComponents and onDestroyed expectations.
- Gun recoil and hit impulses on the hull: wanted for feel? There is no recoil anywhere today.
