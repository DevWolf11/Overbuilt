# WP16: detailed ship models — the design

Base: the update's branch at 727e2d2 (frozen copy: `wp16/tree`). Research this rests on: `wp16/look.md` (today's models
looked at and measured, 197 screenshots in `wp16/look/shots/`), `wp16/code.md` (the pipeline to the line, what may
change), `wp16/real.md` (the real vessels, their kit, colours, how games make ships detailed and cheap). Line numbers are
the frozen tree's (`file:line`) and drift; function names are the stable reference. Real names appear in this note only;
nothing player-visible uses them. Revised after the sceptic's critique (`wp16/critique.md`; the research notes look.md, code.md, real.md and critique.md are also in `docs/ships/wp16/`, probes in `wp16/sceptic/`,
mine in `wp16/wp16probe/`); §11 lists each item's disposition. The pre-critique text is `wp16/wp16probe/design.pre-critique.md`.

The owner, in their words: *"the ship models or part models, they are really not detailed at all, and on ships it should
matter, they should be as realistic and as detailed models as there can be, more than our standard, so please full focus
on that once everything else is finished"*. Assumptions given to them: "part models" means the 59 ship parts and the hulls
they make; the detail is looks only; ships get more detail than anything else in the game within what a phone can draw.

## 0. The decisions, in one table

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Technique | **Geometry and vertex colours only.** Every ship part's look is real geometry with its colours (paint baked in, weathering, markings) in its vertices. No textures on ship parts (the 2D-art-as-texture `plated` path is retired for them), no normal maps, no atlas, no alpha testing, no `InstancedMesh`, no `flatShading` (facets by split normals), **no `DoubleSide`** (a program variant: nets, flags and mesh boards are closed thin boxes). |
| Markings | **Never a coplanar overlay.** Markings are cut into the surface's own triangles (Sutherland-Hodgman, as the boot-top already is), projected per face, coloured per region. A strong-contrast feature is at least 1 css px wide at the entry of the lowest tier that draws it (§3.1a); seams are tone steps between plates, not dark strips. Hull numbers and the transom name are per-vessel **label patches** (only the loft triangles their box touches), never in a model's cache key. |
| Materials | Two new kinds, both the program ships already compile: `shipVC` (vertex colours, everything opaque) and `shipCamo` (vertex colours × the camouflage map, painted surfaces on a camouflage skin only); glass stays `glass`. **No new shader program.** |
| Classes as runs | Every ship piece has a class: `midOnly` (drawn at T2 only, swapped at T1 for its `near` twin: the hull's plain loft, the helideck's plain top), `mid` (main shapes, T2 up), `near` (≥ 0.2 m details, T1 up), `fine` (rails, rungs, wires, T0). A node's geometry per kind is **one buffer whose index is ordered `[midOnly | mid | near | fine]`**: a tier is a `drawRange`, the shadow pass draws `[midOnly | mid]` (swapped in by `onBeforeShadow`/`onAfterShadow`, render3d's city pattern at :3866). Near, fine and glass never cast. A `tall` flag marks thin rods (≤ 6 cm) allowed above a box. |
| LOD | Four tiers. T1-T3 by **CSS pixels per metre** at the ship's nearest point (the current FOV, so the gunner's sight zooms detail in): T1 ≥ 2, T2 ≥ 0.7, T3 below (High); Low: T1 ≥ 3, T2 ≥ 1.1, no T0. T0 by **device pixels per metre** ≥ 20 (a 4 cm lifeline 0.8 device px), per 40 m chunk. 15 % hysteresis. The hangar draws `[mid | near | fine]` always. The phone's default chase of the Arley (466 m, 2.35 px/m) is T1 on High. |
| Draw calls | In flight, a kind-`ship` vessel's static pieces are **merged into one body mesh per material** (the class runs concatenated), plus glass, ≤ 4 fine chunks, one lid mesh per launcher, ≤ 2 label patches; moving rigs keep one mesh per moving node (runs inside). The Arley goes from 72 meshes (×2 with shadows) to ≤ 22 draws and ≤ 10 shadow draws at T1, 1 draw far. |
| Triangles | The Arley ≤ 92 k at T1 (6× today), ≤ 128 k at T0, ≤ 28 k at T2, ≤ 4 k at T3, allocated to b, c and d so the sums fit (§6.3). Smaller ships in proportion. |
| Hull and superstructure dressing | Generated from each part's own data and placed **per vessel**, culled against every other part's box: `shHullDress`/`shipDress` (hulls: rails, bulwark, bitts, anchors, appendages; WP16b) and `superDress` (roof-edge rails, ladders between levels, roof whips; WP16c). The loft surface stays `shullTris` exactly; only its colours, normals and cuts change. |
| Context | Per part: the vessel's `stealth`, `aegis` (two or more Vertical Launchers and no Helicopter Hangar), and per-part `nb` (which of its four walls a neighbour butts), `bay` (the deckhouse nearest amidships carries the boats), `egg` (the forward-most Fire-Control Radar of a ship without `aegis`/`stealth`). Kept per part object in a WeakMap (a split inherits it). Text (number, name) is never in a model's key. |
| Builds | Models of every template the flight can bring in are built at load (`S.warm`); any other ship's models, its dressing, label patches and its near/fine layers are built in `runJobs` slices (≤ 3 ms, `jobLate()` inside loops) when first needed; a ship whose models aren't ready draws its far mesh (built from defs alone). |
| Hits | **What is hit is what is drawn** holds for superstructure too: each side and top face of a building's box is ≥ 85 % covered by mid geometry within 0.5 m; roofs are at the box top; a part resting on another meets drawn geometry within 2 cm. |
| Cosmetic parts | **None added.** Life rafts, boats in bays, searchlights, whips, yards, ladders, flags, rails, bollards and anchors come with the parts and the dressing. The catalogue stays at 59 ship parts. |
| Stock ships | Reshaped **only where the layout itself reads wrong**: parts moved, never added or removed, mass and price unchanged, each move listed and its own baseline keys re-baselined (WP16e, last, droppable). |
| Packages | a1 contract (gates the rest) → a2 pipeline, b hulls, c superstructure/drive/kit, d weapons/sensors in parallel → **a3 integration** (the merge on the final models) → e stock ships and proof. |

## 1. What the player should see

**At 1.5 km** (a speck to 100 px): a grey ship with her silhouette right — the flared bow rising, the blocks of her
superstructure, masts and funnels, the gun's spike, the black boot-top line — and the funnels' black tops (far meshes
keep their top band's colour).

**In the default views** (a destroyer's chase on a desktop is 195 m at 6.8 px/m; on a phone in portrait the follow camera
stands 466 m off at 2.35 px/m: both T1 on High): the window bands, the hull number on the bow, the gun house's flat facets
and its long barrel, the close-in gun's white dome, the dark grids of the launch-cell hatches, boats in their bays, life-raft
racks on the walls, radars that look like radars (an open-mesh rectangle turning, a slotted bar, a dish on a yoke or a white
egg), the destroyer's four flat array faces and her raked tripod mast, rust streaks under the hawse pipes, scupper stains
down the side, a weathered boot-top. On Low the phone's Arley chase is T2: boats, raft racks, yards and domes are mid, so
they still show; only the near fittings go.

**At 30-80 m** (T0: the guns' camera on small ships, the bridge view, a boat's chase, any ship from close by): rails with
three lifelines, bollards and fairleads, anchor chain to the windlass, watertight doors with their dogs, ladders, louvred
vents, the bridge's recessed windows with glass, mullions and wipers, searchlights, whip antennas, yards with lights, the
helideck's markings and nets, the plates' tones, the ship's name across her transom (the name shows from T1 once its
strokes reach a device pixel).

**Grading rubric** for reviewers (look.md's scale): 0 a box with art pasted on; 1 a few primitives; 2 a recognisable built
model; 3 our standard (shape, fittings, markings, weathering). WP16's target: **every part seen at sea grade 3 at 30 m**;
the bridge, deckhouse, funnel, lattice mast, gun house, guns, close-in gun and the hulls beyond the jets (the jets have
fittings and markings; ships must add weathering and the fittings a ship has that a jet hasn't).

## 2. The approach and why

Facts from the research: a ship today is 3-15 k triangles in a frame of 185-222 k; hiding her changes the frame time by
less than the rig's noise; the cost is **draw calls**: about 2 per mesh (main and shadow pass), 54 materials for 72 meshes
on the Arley, no LOD (at 600 m she still costs 176 draws). The superstructure is 12-22 triangle boxes wearing their 2D
drawings as textures (the helideck at 2.3 px/m); the guns are the tank's; the radars a 0.34 m strip.

So detail is cheap only if it comes **merged and tiered**:
1. **Geometry, not texture.** Textures can't merge across parts without an atlas, and an atlas plus camouflage would need a
   second texture (a new program). Vertex colours merge freely and cost nothing per draw, and every ship part can be built
   in node (no canvas), so budgets and determinism are node-testable. They get no mip filtering, so cut-in colour obeys a
   minimum width per tier (§3.1a): it reads cleanly in the tiers that draw it, and nowhere else.
2. **Cut-in colour, not decals.** Our depth buffer is logarithmic: an 8 mm overlay on a hull z-fights by 1 km. Clipping the
   surface's triangles along a marking's edges (the boot-top's method) is exact at any range and draws nothing extra.
3. **Merge per vessel, per material; tiers as draw ranges.** One `shipVC` body mesh holds every static piece's class runs
   in order, so a tier and the shadow pass are each one `drawRange` of one draw; moving rigs keep one mesh per moving node.
   That is ~20 draws for the most complex ship, whatever her triangle count, and no extra draw for a tier.
4. **Tier by pixels per metre.** It tracks what a detail actually covers on screen, works for every size of ship with one
   table, and follows the gunner's sight's zoom for free (TANH). T0 (thin rods) counts device pixels, because whether a 4 cm
   rail crawls depends on the real pixels.
5. **Lazy and safe.** The merge is an optimisation layer over the existing per-part rigs: the rigs are always built, a
   layer not merged yet simply shows its parts' own meshes, so correctness never waits on it and the picture doesn't pop
   when it lands (tested pixel-equal on a paused frame). Model builds and dressing never block a frame in flight.

Rejected: normal maps (memory and a texture sample per pixel on phones; desktop-High extra at most, later); InstancedMesh
for fittings (the instancing program variant per material; merging gives the same one draw); a smoother drawn hull (it would
part from the hit surface by the bilge sagitta: SPEC §8.3's "what is hit is what is drawn, exactly"); alpha-tested rail
strips (vanish at range, costly on mobile GPUs); new cosmetic catalogue parts (mass, hit boxes, builder clutter, saves); a
uniform colour grid on the hull (probe4b: 8-9 k triangles an X segment, 51 k for the Arley's hull).

### 3.1a Classes, widths and relief (every package)

- **Runs.** A recipe emits `[geo, kind, cls, flags]`; `model()` concatenates per kind in class order `[midOnly | mid | near |
  fine]`. A `midOnly` piece always has a `near` twin over the same planes (the hull's plain loft and its cut loft; the
  helideck's plain top and its marked top), so nothing moves at the swap. No recess in `mid` without what fills it (a boat
  bay's boat is mid; a window recess's dark back is mid).
- **Minimum width of cut-in colour.** A strong-contrast strip or stroke (ΔL* > 15) is at least 1 device px wide on the
  phone (DPR 1.75) and 0.5 css px on a DPR-1 desktop at the entry of the lowest tier that draws it: ≥ 0.8 m in a
  `mid`/`midOnly` run (0.7 px/m; today's 0.8 m boot-top band on an X hull is the reference that reads acceptably), ≥ 0.3 m
  in `near` (2 px/m). Weak features (ΔL* ≤ 15: plate tones, wear, streaks, non-skid fields) ≥ 0.3 m. Tone steps (an edge
  between two regions) have no width. Label patches switch on by their own stroke (≥ 1 device px). Today's bands (the
  boot-top, the patrol bow stripe) stay as they are.
- **Relief.** Nothing proud or recessed by less than 3 cm (porthole rims, bolt heads, lifting eyes and strake strips are
  colour or nothing). Fine rods: stanchions ≥ 6 cm, lifelines and rungs ≥ 4 cm, whips ≥ 3 cm at the base.
- **`tall` rods** (whips, ensign and jack staffs, a RIB's arch tubes; ≤ 6 cm Ø, heads ≤ 0.4 m: a light, a small radome, a
  flag) may stand outside their part's box and above the deck cap, at most 6 m; never picked or hit (`userData.noPick`),
  never casting; a listed exemption of 3e/3f.
- **Hits.** Buildings (`bridgeM`, `bridgeL`, `deckhouseL`, `stealthHouse`, `hangar`, `funnel`, `consoleS`) keep their
  top at the box top and each side and top face ≥ 85 % covered by `mid`/`midOnly` surface within 0.5 m of it (a 0.25 m
  raster), so the open area a box keeps is bounded; no recess deeper than 2.4 m. Masts, radars, guns and launchers are frames already (today's lattice mast is a
  truss whose box is mostly air) and are exempt. Inner parts are never drawn in flight (parts3d.js:2338).

### 3.3 Per part (all 59)

Columns: **mid** (T2 up, casts shadows) · **near** (T1 up) · **fine** (T0) · **colour** (cut into the surfaces) · **far**
(T3 silhouette, built from the def alone) · **2D** (what the side view gains, from the same layout function) · **limits**
(what must not move). "Inside its box" means builder-shapes 3e (within 1.5 cm at rest; `tall` rods exempt); "inside its
outline" 3c. Every recipe is gated on `d.ship` (nothing old changes). Paint: walls and vertical faces the skin's base
(`SC.paint(S)`), horizontal walking surfaces deck grey, fittings their own colours.

## 4. Hull dressing and the lofts (WP16b)

### 4.1 Inputs and `shHullDress`

**Inputs, all from the hull's own data**: `shullOf(d).rings` (x, yd, yk, bd, bw at every ring), `d.shull.ends` (closed
bow: stem and anchors; closed stern: transom fittings; joint ends: runs continue; ramp), `d.shull.cls` (S, M, L, X, one:
the spacings and sizes of §3.1), `shullBand(d)` (the scale), `d.shull.bk` (bilge keels), `d.shull.step`, `d.shull.tubes`,
the design waterline `wl`, `shullTris(d)`'s face codes (−6 deck, −7 side and a closed end's plate, −8 bottom, −9 a joint's
end) and its `cap` ranges (the transom is the aft cap of a closed stern). Nothing else, so a player's hull of any class
gets the same.

**`shHullDress(d)`** (parts.js, pure, cached per def, beside `shTubeLay`): the per-segment fitting list in the part's
frame: the deck-edge polylines per side; rail runs `[x0, x1, side, h, n, step]`; bulwark spans; bitts, fairleads, cleats,
hatches and vents `{ k, x, y, z, rot, foot: [x0, x1, z0, z1, y0, y1] }`; anchor gear; transom items; bilge-keel spans;
scupper x's; the colour cuts (strake heights, frame x's, knuckle line); the label regions (the number's box on each bow
side, the name's box on the transom). Hashes of x and the def key choose variants (vent kinds); no random stream.

### 4.2 The two lofts (`shipHullGeo`, which must keep calling `shullTris(d)` first: ships-damage #1)

- **midOnly**: today's loft (its band pieces, ≈ 1.2-1.3 k triangles a segment) with two changes: on flared topsides (bd >
  bw by 5 % or more) the normals are **bent** from a near-vertical wall normal at the waterline to a tilted one at the deck
  edge (Gouraud reads a curved flare), and joint-end caps (code −9) take the side's colour and normal so a joint never shows
  light (look.md #5). It casts the hull's shadow at every tier.
- **near**: `shullTris`' triangles cut **in their own planes**, projected per face: sides (−7, −8) in the side projection
  (x, y) per sign of z, the deck (−6) in plan (x, z), the transom (the aft cap) in the end view (z, y). Cuts: the strake
  edges and frames as single lines (tone steps), the knuckle line, the boot-top's two edges and its ragged top, streak
  strips (≥ 0.3 m), rust fans, salt, the deck's non-skid fields and margin, the stealth grid. **No uniform grid.** Each piece
  is coloured by its **region** (which plate, field or streak it lies in; vertices are shared only within a region), with
  smooth gradients (wear, salt) on top. Every piece stays in its triangle's plane, so **the surface is `shullTris`
  exactly** (hits, decals, buoyancy untouched). The triangles any label region touches are put **last** in the index, so a
  `drawRange` can leave them out where a label patch draws them (§4.4).
- **Budgets per segment** (probe `wp16probe/probe5-single.js`: single-line steps alone give X 2.2-2.9 k, L 2.3-2.5 k, M
  0.9-1.5 k, S 0.6 k, one-piece 0.8 k): near loft X ≤ 4.5 k (bow) / 3.5 k, L ≤ 3.8 / 3.0 k, M ≤ 2.2 / 1.8 k, S ≤ 1.0 k,
  one-piece ≤ 1.5 k; midOnly as today (≤ 1.4 k). Indexed. Build ≤ 12 ms desktop for an X bow, sliced with `jobLate()` when
  built in flight.

### 4.3 `shipDress(vm, parts)` (parts3d.js, WP16b's region)

Called by `VesselMesh` whenever its layout signature changes, as `pylons()` is; in flight as a job when the ship first
reaches T1 (never at first sight); in the hangar on every rebuild (the dressing follows every edit).
1. each hull part's list into the vessel frame;
2. **culling**: every other part's box (part rect × depth, +0.2 m) is a blocker: a fitting whose footprint meets one is
   dropped; rail runs are split at blocked intervals (end stanchions at each break, stubs under 1.5 m dropped), joined
   across joints, replaced by nets along a helideck's span and by the bulwark where it runs;
3. **style** from the vessel's ctx: stealth drops rails, exposed bitts and anchors (flush covers in the loft's colours);
4. **appendages**: for each `screwM`/`screwL`, a shaft from its hub forward to where it enters the hull's bottom (found on
   the rings) and an A-bracket of two struts to a boss just ahead of the screw;
5. pieces `[geo, kind, cls, flags]` from the library, hung as `rig.dress` on the hull part they belong to (appendages on
   the screw's rig) so `prune`/`freeRig` free them with it and the merge and the bake walk them.
Cost: ≤ 30 k triangles on the Arley (near ≤ 12 k, fine ≤ 18 k), built ≤ 15 ms desktop, sliced.

### 4.4 Numbers, the name, label patches

**Number** `shipNumber(parts, salt)` (parts.js): a hash of the **sorted multiset of part types** (no positions, so every
screen agrees whatever the spawn rows' rounding), salted by the owner's room id for a player's craft in a room (peers
agree on it) or by the AI ship's order of first sight among the flight's ships of that design (local only: AI is off in
rooms), 0 solo; 100-899 for L and X, 10-89 for M; never the class lead's real number (51, 7, 31, 1: salted away).

**Name**: the design's name — `v.name` for the player's own ship, `Builder.name` in the hangar, and for a peer the spawn
message's `name` stored on `P.design` (one mp.js line, `// [WP16a]`; today `vesselOf` names a ghost after the player, so
the transom would differ per screen); sanitised to `[A-Z0-9 -]`, upper-cased, the first two words, ≤ 14 characters; on
closed sterns of M, L and X. Peer text becomes geometry only, never HTML.

**Label patch**: per vessel, the label region's triangles of that hull part's near loft, cut by the glyphs (`SH_FONT`:
digits and capitals as convex quads, shared with 2D) and coloured; port-side glyphs laid out to read left to right from
outside. While a patch shows, its loft's `drawRange` stops before the label-region tail; otherwise the plain tail draws.
A patch is on when its stroke is ≥ 1 device px (number 0.3 m strokes: ≥ 3.3 device px/m; X name 0.15 m: ≥ 6.7), off 15 %
below, and only where the near loft draws. Patches live in an LRU (16), are built as jobs, and never enter a model's key.

### 4.5 2D

`shullDraw` draws the side-visible dressing from `shHullDress` (rails as stanchions and lifelines on the sheer, the bulwark,
bitts, the anchor (today's), the number, the name on the transom's edge view, the knuckle line, today's draft marks) and,
when `PartPaint.ship` carries the design's parts, culls by them exactly as 3D does; a part drawn alone (the tray) shows the
uncut runs.

## 5. Model context (`shipCtx`, WP16a)

`shipCtx(parts, salt)` (parts.js, pure): the vessel's fields `{ stealth: any part with def.stealth, aegis: count(vls8) ≥ 2
&& !any hangar, no: shipNumber(parts, salt), name }` and, through `shipPartCtx(p, parts, vctx)`, each part's own: `nb`
(4 bits in the part's own frame, rot and flip undone: its fore, aft, port, starboard wall butted by another part's box
within 0.3 m over ≥ 50 % of the face), `bay` (1 for the `deckhouseL` whose centre is nearest the vessel's mid-length, 0
for the others), `egg` (1 for the forward-most `nRadarFC` on a ship without `aegis` or `stealth`). `VesselMesh` computes
them once and keeps them per part object in a WeakMap (looks only, never saved or sent); a vessel made by a split takes its
parts' entries as they are, so nothing rebuilds and the number doesn't change when a ship breaks. The hangar recomputes
them on every rebuild.

`CTX_USE` (parts3d.js) is declared once, empty, in a1's region; each package's region adds its rows with
`Object.assign(CTX_USE, {…})`: b `shull` closed bow and closed stern: stealth; c `bridgeL`, `house`, `hangar`: aegis,
stealth, nb (and `house`: bay), `bridge` (Wheelhouse): nb, `lattice`: aegis; d `nvAsm`, `nvTorp`, `nvDecoy`, naval
`tradar`: stealth (`tradar` track: also egg). Only those fields enter the model's cache key (≤ 2 × 16 × 2 variants of
a deckhouse in theory, a handful in practice); text never does. The builder's 2D draw (builder.js, the part loop, ~:3736)
and World's 2D vessel draw (world.js:966) set `PartPaint.ship = { ...shipCtx(parts), parts, of: (p) => shipPartCtx(p, parts,
vctx) }` while drawing a design that has a ship part, and clear it after.

Which stock ships get what: stealth — Vizby; aegis — Arley (two VLS, no hangar); the Olivar (two VLS and a hangar) keeps
the Perry's slab sides and her forward FC radar becomes the egg; the Pegazus' and the Osca's FC radars are eggs; `bay` on the
Arley's aft deckhouse (x −12) and the Olivar's at x 0.

ratio (it changes at most every 1.5 s: PR #49). Computed in `syncVessels` (render3d:6263) beside today's `d`, put on

### 6.2 The ship layer (the merge)

Only for vessels of kind `ship` in flight (`VesselMesh(parts, { ship: true, name, salt })` from `syncVessels`); the
Catalyna (a plane) and any other kind with a ship part draw their parts' meshes as rigs, unmerged. Old parts on a ship (the
Cyclon's and Osca's turrets and MGs) stay rigs, exactly as they draw today, except at T3 where the far mesh carries their
box.
- **Runs everywhere.** Every model node's geometry per kind is indexed `[midOnly | mid | near | fine]`
  (`geo.userData.runs`); a rig mesh's tier is a `drawRange`; its shadow range `[midOnly | mid]` is swapped in by
  `onBeforeShadow` and restored by `onAfterShadow` (three returns before any GL draw at count 0: render3d's city stand-ins,
  :3866). A mesh with no `midOnly`/`mid` run never casts (glass, near-only pieces).
- **Static** = a ship part's mesh not under a moving node (`yaw`, `pitch`, `recoil`, `antenna`, `shScrew`, `shRud`,
  `shOut`, `shJet`, `shRamp`, `nvYaw`, `nvPitch`, `nvSpin`) and whose part isn't ring-borne (`tgOf`); `rig.dress` and
  `superDress` too.
- **Layers**: `body` (shipVC: every static piece's runs concatenated by class, so `[all midOnly | all mid | all near-rest |
  all label-region tails]`), `camo` (the same, one per camouflage skin aboard; each part's camouflage offset, `camoMap(S,
  at)`'s per-part `p.y` offset, baked into its UVs so the pattern doesn't jump), `glass`, `fine` chunks (≤ 40 m of ship
  length each, ≤ 4 on the Arley), `caps` (each launcher's `nvCap<k>` lids merged in order with their triangle runs:
  `drawRange` = the first `p.ammo` lids, one draw per launcher), `labels` (≤ 2 patches), and `far` (decimated hull + each
  part's far silhouette from its def and layout: no model build). All in the group's frame (part space), children of
  `vm.group` (water.mjs hides `vm.group`).
- **Compact**: merged layers use `Uint8` normalised colours, `Int8` normalised normals and `Uint16` indices where they fit
  (same program); their CPU position, normal and colour arrays are released after upload (the index is kept for
  collapses). Rig geometry stays as built (the bake reads it).
- **Built lazily**, one layer per job, ≤ 3 ms of merging a frame across all ships (most visible first), and at most one
  layer's first draw (its upload, ≤ 1.5 MB) per frame; `far` at first sight, `body` (midOnly and mid runs) and `caps` when
  she first reaches T2, the near runs, glass, labels and dressing when she first reaches T1, `fine` at T0. Until a layer
  exists its parts' own meshes draw in its place (same pixels); once it exists they're hidden (`userData.merged`).
- **Freed**: near runs (the body is rebuilt without them), glass, labels and fine chunks are freed after 30 s at T2 or
  lower and rebuilt as jobs on return.
- **Merged statics' rigs** skip `update()` (they don't move): less CPU than today.
- **Part lost** (`prune`): its triangle runs in every layer are collapsed (indices set degenerate, a partial index
  update), no rebuild.
- **Picking**: hidden-because-merged meshes stay pickable (their world matrices are kept); `vm.pick` treats
  `userData.merged` as shown; `noPick` pieces (`tall`) are skipped.
- **Wreck**: `char` reaches the layers' materials once; the bake folds **only the rigs** into one mesh, walking them
  whatever the tier (`bakeList` ignores a tier's hiding, `userData.tierHidden`, never other hiding), and keeps the merged
  layers as they are (charred, still switched by tier), so a wreck baked at T3 keeps her guns and the player's own wreck
  keeps its detail in the end camera; ≤ 15 ms (desktop) for an Arley's rigs.
- **Shadows**: the body's `[midOnly | mid]` and above-water rig meshes whose bounding radius is ≥ 1 m at T1/T0 or ≥ 2 m at
  T2 (smaller ones never: a ship-only rule; tanks unchanged); near, fine, glass, caps, labels, under-water rigs and far
  never.

**Builds in flight.** At the flight's load (`S.warm`) the models of the player's ship and of every template ship.js can
bring into this flight (its Range and Practice rosters, read, never written) are built and cached per (def, skin, ctx
fields). A ship whose models aren't all cached (a peer's new design) is drawn by a far-only stub (`vm.farOnly`) while its
part models build as jobs (a part per slice; the hull's near cut sliced inside with `jobLate()`); a2 checks that every
reader of `vessels.get(v).vm` copes with the stub. No model build, dressing, label or merge runs synchronously at first
sight in flight.

## 10. Risks and fallbacks

- **The merge is subtle code in `VesselMesh`.** It is an optimisation over rigs that always exist; if a2 slips, b, c, d's
  models still draw right (more draws) and a3 reports the budgets unmet rather than shipping a broken merge.
- **The shadow swap** (`onBeforeShadow` ranges) is the city's proven pattern; if a phone's driver mishandles a range
  change between passes, the fallback is a second, count-0 mesh over the same geometry for the shadow (one more shadow
  draw, none in the main pass).
- **Context variants surprise a builder** (adding two launchers changes the bridge to array faces and the FC radar to a
  dish): it is shown in the hangar at once and in 2D; it reads as a real distinction (area-defence ships wear arrays). If
  the owner dislikes it, `aegis` or `egg` can be dropped in one line each.
- **Coverage rules cost realism**: walls lean ≤ 0.5 m (4-6°), the bridge's wings are enclosed, a boat fills its bay's lower half to the wall's face. If the
  owner prefers the real shapes over shells meeting what is drawn, the 0.5 m rises to 1 m (§12, question 1).
- **Model build time on a phone**: templates build at load and are cached; if the 4× profile exceeds 1 s for a Practice
  battle's roster, the prewarm itself moves into jobs during the loading screen, the far mesh standing in.
- **Time**: if the whole is too big, the priority order is the owner's eye: weapons (olive, radars, guns) → superstructure
  (bridge, funnel, deckhouse, mast) → hull colours and numbers → dressing → the rest.
